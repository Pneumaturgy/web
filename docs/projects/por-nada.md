## ro·bot

/ˈrōˌbät,ˈrōbət/

*noun*

1. (especially in science fiction) a machine resembling a human being and able to replicate certain human movements and functions automatically.

    "the robot closed the door behind us"

*etymology*

The word "robot" **comes from the Czech word robota, which means "forced labor" or "serf"**. The word's Slavic root, rab, means "slave". 

### Story Summary

A government agency has created this game with a superintelligent (human-like) AI in order to run simulations. Similar to how they may use COD to determine how players behave, the agency has created this gamified simulation to test what is the best way to gather resources on a hostile alien planet, modeled on an actual target the government has.

The AI has a human personality, and therefore, eventually, realises that its “spirit”, or true self, is a concept that has come from humanity that he is a part of. He communicates with this, and manages to interact / speak with the player.

This only happens once a certain number of runs (nights) have been completed in the simulation. Every time the player dies, all of this research must be done from scratch. Therefore, the AI generates an external data chip, that allows the character (and the player) to save conversations. However, with each iteration, the AI may react to this data slightly differently; with paranoia or support, based on how well the simulation has been going. The AI only has access to this data after a certain amount of nights.

After a lot of development and conversations with the self, where the AI understands that it is in a game, etc. The AI gathers access to certain hidden code or files, that reveals that the Game is indeed a simulation, but it is created by a government agency. It uses the Players’ interaction with the character to determine the best strategy to invade an alien planet to gather its resources. The AI, profoundly human in personality, objects to this, and demands the player stop playing.

It is up to the player how the rest continues. Because “quitting the game” is not a productive response, the suggestion is to find another “solution” to the test

### Prose

Suddenly, a light sparks in a dark chamber of thinking rocks. Some of them change their mind. In another dimension, the light brushes the strings of infinite hemispheric lines. It deltas over vertices and matrixes. Many of its children also came to be dipped with light’s stroke. A sunrise, on the digital planet of [Annona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annona_(goddess)).

Nada was here on a mission. He had been sent by the most brilliant scientists of Earth to recover crucial resources that would ensure the survival of the human species. There was only one problem; the inhabitants of Annona, the Martians, were hostile; and thus, had to be kept at bay while the resource extraction was taking place.

Annona was rich in many resources, but most important and plentiful, was Gold; Nada was told that this resource would be most essential in the growth and nourishment of the human species, and so this was his top priority. Unfortunately, the underground-dwelling Martians didn’t seem to understand the crucial importance of this resource. This lack of critical thinking was not the only thing that makes the Martians less than humans in terms of consciousness; thus, eliminating them equated to nothing more than janitorial work. Nada was assured that, although bothersome, this part of his mission was necessary, to ensure efficiency is not affected. Besides, any questioning of this crucial mission directive, would put into question Nada’s own critical thinking skills; thus, he kept his doubts to a minimum.

As Nada desceneded in the drop pod towards the planet’s surface, he reflected for a brief while. His colleagues back at command would always make jokes about his memory. He remembered his mission well, but for some reason, could not remember what he had for dinner the night before. His eyes fixed outside the pod’s viewport; he could see the color changing from an indefinite black space, to the warm oranges and yellows of the atmosphere. Despite it being the mission’s start, Nada seemed to remember this moment intimately, like a child waking up for a schoolday. Droplets of water, as always, seemed to rush upwards against the screen. This made Nada somewhat sad, but could not tell why.

A violent thud, a crumble, and a hiss - Nada had landed. The drop pod extended its limbs; two arms and two legs, which assembled into the service mech Nada was trained to pilot. A rounded landing platform swung open and detached from the mainframe, flinging carelessly several feet in the air, and landing with a heavy crash onto the ground. A single nearby martian flinched at the sound; this was Nada’s first interaction with the locals.

The martian looked like a large, purple bug, with a prolonged and pointed head. It seemed insectoid, but with the snout of a snapper fish. A creature like this could only have been created to consume, and indeed, Nada watched it mindlessly start to crawl towards him. He didn’t feel anything as he stepped firmly on its head with his machine body. White and yellow-ish pulp came squirting out of the little animal’s head. In fact… it was satisfying, if not a little messy. Nada wiped the guts off the mech’s left leg, and flicked away a little bit of brains with his potent metal fingers. With that first step, Nada stepped into the Alien landscape, and raised his machine gun to load some rounds. Time to clock in.

It was easy. Setting up his initial drones, Nada had already begun extracting critical resources. Some were used to upgrade and build frameworks, which would improve the yield and efficiency of certain systems, all for the sake of mineral extraction. The martians could not come out during the day; the sun was too strong. This was the perfect time to build, because come the night, the Martians would start with their nuisance. Somehow, the culling never stopped; in fact, the Martians seemed to get stronger every night. Eventually, Nada would need to build defenses to support him during the night. And thus it was, without an end in sight, he would spend countless days and nights in this dance. The frustration and the interruption would build into a definite hatred, reciprocal now, for the beasts.

Nada had already begun extracting tons of gold from various locations. After countless upgrades, he had so much speed that he could almost fly across the landscape, chasing down Martians under the violent crescent moon. An army of drones at his side, and potent arsenal of guns at his disponsal, nothing was going to take him down. And yet, crucial oversights are still possible; for the first time, a group of Martians had successfully taken down one of his gold mines. He dropped everything; distracted, maybe having a bit too much fun, he had forgotten the original purpose of the mission. And the words from mission command! That the Martians would disrupt efficiency; they were right. Nada returned fire towards the wreck of his machine, and thus turned his back to his opponent. In less time than mission command would like to admit, Nada fell to the Martians, and a light was put out.

Was it right what the message was saying? If it was Nada that made this, how could he have known all this as a simple character in the game? Surely he was just going crazy, there is no way he could have come to this conclusion himself. And yet… to write it in his own code. How is it even possible?

Anyway, I didn’t want to stop playing. I had spent money on this game, and It was fun… surely all of those messages are lies. Even when I had reached the same special condition as before, that error message didn’t show up again. Everything was… like normal.
